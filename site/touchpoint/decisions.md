## The calls that mattered

**Work inside the team, not beside it.** The engagement began with one developer joining the product's own engineering team - their process, their codebase, their standards - rather than ManiarTech taking work away to deliver from a distance. That is why the relationship survived six years and an acquisition: there was never a wall between "the client's team" and "the vendor's team."

**Build on what was inherited.** The original infrastructure was stood up by an earlier engineer. The tempting move for an ambitious new developer is the rewrite; the right move was to master the existing system and improve it from within. The platform evolved continuously - through Backbone and CoffeeScript toward React - without a big-bang rebuild betting the product's continuity.

**Grow the team by training, not just hiring.** When the Strativity acquisition turned the role into a formal offshore partnership, we hired two more engineers and invested in training them properly on the product before they carried it. Strativity's Chief Product Officer singled that training out in his testimonial - along with transparency about time and issues. We would rather surface a slip early than report a comfortable fiction; habits like that, more than any technology choice, are what keep a US product company's trust across six years.
