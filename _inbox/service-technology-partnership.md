## What it is — the model (from the LnB briefing)

ManiarTech acts as the **technology partner** in a multi-party venture. The structure is a
**fair "three kinds of capital, none free"** model:

| Partner | Brings | Earns ownership via |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology (ManiarTech)** | Months of skilled build time — a real, unrecoverable opportunity cost | **Valued time** at an agreed rate (funds the build itself) |
| **Network / Capital** | Distribution + cash that funds the shared business fund | Cash at face value + delivered supply |
| **Media / Brand** | Marketing, brand, demand generation | Marketing delivery, earned on results |

Key principle: **"the technology partner is never out of pocket"** — every cash cost (servers,
tools, even during the build) comes from the shared fund; ManiarTech contributes *time*, and
earns equity for it. ManiarTech can bring all three capabilities itself, or slot in as the tech
partner alongside others.

## ★ The fair-commitment model (the showable IP — publishable as "how we partner")

This is the genuinely differentiated, reusable substance — a **fair, de-risked partnership
structure** ManiarTech can offer any venture partner (the *approach*, not the LnB deal):
- **Prove the riskiest thing cheaply, before anyone commits big** (staged, gated — don't fund
  the next stage until the last shows real signs of working). *(Same de-risk discipline as the
  Keystone Method + Legacy Modernization.)*
- **Earned ownership over time (vesting)** — each partner's share builds as they deliver their
  part; step away early, keep only what you genuinely earned. Protects whoever stays committed.
- **Funding secured up front (escrow), released against milestones** — build against money
  that's actually secured, not a promise.
- **Clear written responsibilities; every contribution (incl. time) given a fair recorded value.**
- **Work already done is honored; the code is protected until commitments are met; "backing
  off" is defined in advance** — no bitter disputes.
- **Lean, pay-as-you-grow** — modest tech costs, spend where it's the edge, keep the rest cheap.

> Why it matters: the #1 reason ventures fail isn't the market — it's the partners. ManiarTech's
> answer is a structure that makes *staying committed the rational choice* and protects everyone.
> "Good fences make good partners." This is a real, trust-building offer — and publishable as a
> framework without exposing any deal.
