## You'll know quickly whether we understand a problem like yours

You're not buying a number. You're checking, with low effort and zero commitment, whether the people you'd hand this to actually grasp what you're trying to build. So the read you get back names the **decisions that genuinely change the shape of the work** — the trade-offs a senior engineer would flag — not a generic price pulled from a form.

If we've understood it, you'll feel it in the response. It shows its assumptions, so you can see exactly what we took the estimate to mean. And if it's not a fit, you'll know that fast too — we'll say so.

## How an estimate works

1. **Tell us what you're building.** A custom application, an automation, a system that needs modernizing, an AI feature — in your own words. A few details about scope, timeline, and constraints help.
2. **A senior engineer reviews it.** Not a bot, not a junior — someone who actually scopes work like this. They read your note and reply with a ballpark and the assumptions behind it.
3. **You get a ballpark estimate.** A realistic range for what the work involves, the assumptions it rests on, and a clear next step if you'd like to take it further.

> **It's an estimate, not a final quote.** A real quote comes after we understand your project properly — with a person, in a real conversation. This is the fast, no-pressure first look.

## What to include

The more of this you can share, the sharper the read — but none of it is required. Send what you have:

- **The problem.** What you're trying to solve, and for whom.
- **Rough scope.** The main things the software needs to do.
- **Timeline.** Any dates or milestones you're working toward.
- **Constraints.** Existing systems, a tech stack you're tied to, budget bounds, compliance needs — whatever shapes the work.
