# Recording, and drawing on, live video — entirely in the browser

> Built for a US sports-tech startup: a coach's video-review studio that ran where, in 2019, it
> arguably shouldn't have been able to — inside the browser.

> **Client:** a US sports-technology startup (anonymized) · **Our role:** a small ManiarTech team built
> it from scratch; Aamir led the architecture and the complex core · **When:** ~2019 · **Tech:** WebRTC
> + our own WebDoodling framework · **Outcome:** the engineering shipped and worked; the venture didn't
> find commercial traction (a market outcome, not an engineering one).

## What it was
A platform for a US sports startup — it began as a recruitment service to help players get seen by
coaches and recruiters, then grew toward something harder: letting coaches **review and coach players
through video**, online.

## The hard part: a video studio inside the browser
Coaches needed to review players' match footage *in the browser* — and actively. In the editor we
built, a coach can play the video and slow it down or speed it up, **record voice commentary** over it,
**draw directly on the live, running video** to show a player exactly what to fix, and set markers and
comments at the moments that matter — and **all of it, including the drawing on the moving video, is
recorded live, in the browser.**

In ~2019, that was at the **edge of what browsers could do** — so we treated it like a frontier. We
built a **proof-of-concept first**, to prove the browser could actually support it *before* committing
the full build. For the live capture we used **WebRTC**; for drawing on the running video, we used
**WebDoodling — our own canvas framework.** The challenge was never the design; it was whether the
technology could be pushed that far. We made sure it could before we promised it would.

## How it turned out — straight
The platform did what it was engineered to do. The **venture around it didn't find commercial
traction**, and it was wound down. We're including it anyway, and telling you that plainly, for two
reasons: a case study is meant to show **capability**, and an in-browser video studio at the edge of
2019's technology is a real one — and because we'd rather you trust what we say about the wins because
you've seen us be honest about the ones that didn't pan out.

## Why it matters
- **We push the frontier — carefully.** When a requirement sat at the edge of the technology, we
  **de-risked it with a PoC** before building, instead of promising and hoping.
- **We build on what we make.** The drawing-on-live-video ran on **WebDoodling, our own framework** —
  real "we make our own technology" depth, used in a production product.
- **We're honest about outcomes.** We don't dress up a market failure as a success. If we tell you
  something worked, it's because it did.
