## ⚠️ Honest framing — this is where calibration matters MOST (Governing Rule #1)

"Python is slow, migrate to Go" is a tempting but **over-broad** pitch. The honest, more
credible (and more ManiarTech) version:

- **Go helps for SPECIFIC needs** — high concurrency, performance-critical paths, lower
  memory/compute cost, single-binary deployment, type safety at scale. Frame the value there,
  not as a blanket "Python bad."
- **ManiarTech is POLYGLOT and pragmatic — and that's a credibility asset, not a weakness.**
  ManiarTech itself builds in **Python** (Tallery Gallery, Documentor are Django/Python) AND
  Go (the whole ecosystem). It even modernized Tallery to *newer Python*, not to Go — because
  that was right for that system. So we're **honest brokers who pick the right tool**, not Go
  zealots. → Pitch: *"we migrate to Go when your workload genuinely benefits — and we'll tell
  you when it doesn't."* That honesty (echoing the house "no output better than wrong output"
  ethos) builds more trust than absolutist speed claims.
- ❌ **AVOID:** "Python is slow/legacy" as a blanket put-down; guaranteed performance multiples
  ("10× faster"); implying every system should move to Go; unverified before/after benchmarks.
- ✅ **TRUE / safe:** "We migrate performance- and cost-sensitive systems to Go, safely and
  incrementally, backed by deep Go authorship (libraries, tooling, and the Indigo language)."
  Any performance claim must be **reproducible, method shown**.
