# ⭐ PRESENTATION DOCTRINE — How to Present ManiarTech
### (CANONICAL — governs ALL client-facing material: website, sales presentations, proposals, LinkedIn, one-pagers, pitch decks, cold emails)

> **Read this BEFORE creating or enhancing any material that represents ManiarTech to a
> prospective client.** Born from the root-cause diagnosis (2026-06, confirmed by Aamir + a cold
> 3-persona reviewer panel): **ManiarTech's low client results were a PRESENTATION problem, not a
> substance problem.** The substance is world-class and done. This doctrine is the fix, made
> permanent. It pairs with two gates that also apply to everything: the **Claim Audit**
> (TRUST-STRATEGY.md) and the **Skeptic Test** (customer-lens-review.md).

---

## ★★★ THE CORE PRINCIPLE

# LEAD WITH THEIR SAFETY, NOT YOUR BRILLIANCE.
## Same substance — opposite order.

Open with the **client's problem**. Prove you **deliver**. Make it feel **safe**. Lead credibility
with the **pedigree a skeptic respects** (JP Morgan). And let your **depth** — the standards, the
language, "we make technology" — be the **closer that seals trust, never the opener that invites
suspicion.**

> **WHY:** To a buyer who can't evaluate craft and just needs to feel safe, leading with depth
> reads as *risky / unproven / one-person / lock-in / not-for-me.* Engineers and buyers need
> **opposite** things in the first 30 seconds. The deeper the builder, the stronger the urge to
> lead with depth — and the harder it backfires. This is the shadow of strength, not a flaw.

---

## THE PRESENTATION ORDER  *(apply to any format — page, deck, proposal, post)*
1. **THEIR problem / outcome** — they see themselves in 5 seconds.
2. **Proof you DELIVER** — client case studies / outcomes / references *(the #1 trust currency)*.
3. **Why it's SAFE** — senior-only · no lock-in · right-sized · ISO process · straight talk.
4. **Credibility** — lead with **JP Morgan**; then founder + team + process.
5. **Depth (the CLOSER)** — standards / Indigo / "we make it" — framed as **benefit + no-lock-in + verifiable.**
6. **Low-risk next step** — free estimate / small pilot / "talk to a human."

---

## PRIORITY HIERARCHY — what leads, what supports, what closes
- **LEAD WITH:** the client's problem · client proof · safety/de-risk · the **JP Morgan** pedigree.
- **SUPPORT WITH:** ISO/process · senior-only · honest-engineering · verifiable specifics.
- **CLOSE WITH (never open):** the standards · Indigo · the Labs · "we make technology" · the "research lab" identity.
- **ALWAYS THREAD THROUGH:** verifiability (every big claim one click from proof) · no-lock-in reassurance · plain language · honest hedges.

---

## ❌ DON'T  *(the traps that have cost us clients — from the cold-panel critique)*
- **Lead with "we make technology / five standards / a programming language."** From a company they've never heard of, it sounds *inflated*, not impressive ("if it were real, I'd have heard of them").
- **Call self-authored specs "open standards" baldly.** Overclaiming makes them re-scrutinize *everything* on the page. Say only what's verifiable.
- **Headline star counts** ("325★"). Modest to a developer, meaningless to a buyer. Let it support, never lead.
- **Use jargon** — "agentic AI," "expert-quality Go," "knowledge-grounded." Buzzword bingo destroys trust.
- **Make "research lab" the front-door identity.** It reads to buyers as expensive / slow / academic / "I don't want to fund your research."
- **Flex Countrywide** ("world's leading home-loan provider / Most Admired Fortune 500"). It's the 2008-crisis name — tone-deaf to US/EU buyers. **Lead JP Morgan; Countrywide a plain list item at most.**
- **Talk about US before THEM.** **Pretend to be a bigger team.** **State "we decline unethical work" as an unprompted headline** (makes them wonder who you've worked with).
- **Make any claim you can't render verifiable.** Unverifiable big claims hurt more than help here.

## ✅ DO  *(what actually builds trust)*
- **Open with the customer's problem in their words.** Let them see themselves fast.
- **Show client proof early** — situations, what you built, real outcomes (anonymized if needed).
- **De-risk explicitly:** senior-only ("we don't learn on your project") · **no lock-in** ("mainstream, hireable tech — Go/Python/React/PostgreSQL") · right-sized ("not a science experiment") · ISO process · first-pass quality.
- **Lead credibility with JP Morgan** + the verifiable 27-year track record.
- **Frame depth as benefit to THEM** + verifiable + no lock-in ("the rigor your project inherits; you're never locked into it; check it yourself").
- **Plain language. Honest hedges** ("most of the time," not "always"). **Offer a human** + a low-commitment first step.

## KNOW YOUR AUDIENCE  *(the one exception to buyer-first)*
This doctrine is for **prospective CLIENTS** — most sales material. For **recruiting / developer /
peer / OSS-community** audiences (hiring posts, conference talks, Show HN / r/golang launches),
**depth-first is correct** — engineers *want* the standards and the language up front, and there
the "we make technology" story is the magnet. **Match the order to the audience.** When in doubt
for anything sales-facing: **buyer-first.**
