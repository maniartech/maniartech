heading: "Gamification & engagement - done from the research, not the app store"
---

Enterprise software has an adoption problem: the system is bought by a director and abandoned by the staff. Gamification is the usual prescription - and done the app-store way (points, badges, leaderboards), it reliably backfires: controlled studies show public ranking and points economies *reduce* motivation in workplace settings. We know this literature well enough to have written a white paper on it - [read it before you buy gamification from anyone](/white-papers/gamification-enterprise-motivation/), including us.

What actually works is engagement engineering around the three levers the research supports: **competence** (visible progress on real work - a completeness meter on a record, a queue that empties), **autonomy** (the system serves the worker's judgment instead of surveilling it), and **connection** (team-level goals rather than person-vs-person rankings). We design those into the workflow itself - which is why this is a capability of the enterprise practice and not a plugin. And per the paper's own conclusion, we will tell you when the honest fix is not gamification at all: no badge fixes a broken workflow.
