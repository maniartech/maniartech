---
heading: "Advise - architecture review with a written verdict"
---

Sometimes the right engagement is an expert eye, not a build team. An advisory engagement produces a **written review you keep**: findings ranked by actual risk, not by how interesting they are to engineers; the quick wins separated from the structural work; a fix / modernize / leave-alone call on each part (leave-alone is a real category - code that is ugly but stable and rarely touched is usually not your problem); and where it applies, an honest build-vs-buy recommendation, including "an off-the-shelf product fits you better."

We look at what determines an application's future: whether the architecture can carry the next three years of your roadmap, where performance will break first, how access control and data handling would look to a security review, and how hard the system would be for a new team to take over - because that last one is the real measure of lock-in. It is the difference between hiring coders and hiring an engineering partner: coders add code; a partner will tell you to stop.
