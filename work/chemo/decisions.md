## The calls that mattered

**Build on Processious - and say so honestly, even though it meant waiting.** The client wanted a system immediately; our process-automation platform was still being matured for exactly this class of work. We told them straight, and proposed building on it anyway rather than hand-rolling another one-off. The cost was real - they waited about a year. What they got in return is a lab system on a platform whose whole job is governed, auditable workflow, with room to grow module by module.

**Make report delivery recipient-bound, not just link-protected.** An emailed link is a leak waiting to happen: links get forwarded. So delivery requires the recipient to know report-specific details, and the one-time password goes only to the customer-authorized user - a forwarded link fails by design. The trade-off is extra friction for legitimate recipients. For confidential, regulated lab data, we judged that friction correct.

**Put the authenticity checker in public.** The report checker is open to anyone, not just customers - which means maintaining a public-facing surface for the lab. We made that call because a certificate's value depends on a stranger being able to trust it: a regulator, an auditor, a buyer.

**Reuse the workflow-at-a-glance design that had already survived a decade.** The staged-timeline view - every sample's state, stamped with who and when - is the same design DNA as the lab system we built years earlier, which is what these users knew and asked for. We kept what was proven and rebuilt the platform underneath it.
