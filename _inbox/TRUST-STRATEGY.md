# Trust Strategy — the two questions the website MUST answer

> ## ⛔ GOVERNING RULE #1 — NOWHERE EXAGGERATE OR OVERPROMISE (Aamir, emphatic)
> Zero exaggeration. Zero overpromising. **Every claim must be TRUE, SOURCED, and CALIBRATED.**
> This rule overrides every other consideration — if a claim is bold but can't be backed, it
> gets cut or downgraded, never inflated. Operational guarantee = the CLAIM AUDIT below.
>
> **CLAIM AUDIT — every claim, every page, before it ships:**
> 1. **True?** — factually correct, right now.
> 2. **Sourced?** — traceable to a dossier / real number / verifiable artifact.
> 3. **Calibrated?** — no inflation; specific provable fact, not an unbacked superlative.
> 4. **Status-honest?** — the project's label (Published/Beta/Shipping soon/Research/Internal)
>    matches reality; no "production-ready" for beta, no install CTA for unpublished.
>
> **Banned:** unbacked superlatives (world-class/best/leading/fastest/cutting-edge); invented
> or misleadingly-rounded numbers; fake scale (team/clients/stars/offices); claiming track
> record we don't have; non-reproducible benchmark/competitor claims; promising outcomes we
> can't deliver. **Allowed:** bold framing of REAL substance ("larger than life without lying"),
> "we CAN build X" backed by Labs/products, honest ranges ("~60% faster"), reproducible
> benchmarks with method shown. Why: for an offshore-trust-gap brand, ONE caught exaggeration
> discredits everything; honesty IS the moat; the truth is already impressive — show it, never
> inflate it.

## RISK 1 — "Almost everything is pre-1.0 or unpublished."
**The silent fear:** *"Impressive demos, but can they ship? Will this be abandoned? Is any
of it real and usable today?"* Brilliance the world can't see or use doesn't compound, and
to a cold visitor, unfinished reads as risky.

### How the site answers it (antidotes)
1. **Lead with what IS shipped and provable.** Put the published, usable, star-bearing work
   first and loudest: **signals (~325★), vault-storage (v2), gotime (v2), Internet Object
   (live spec + parsers + playground)**. Proof that ManiarTech ships and maintains real,
   adopted software. The "coming soon" work is the *pipeline*, not the *proof*.
2. **Honest status labels, everywhere, no exceptions.** A fixed vocabulary —
   **Published · Beta · Shipping soon · Research (design-phase) · Internal** — one per
   project. Truth-in-labeling is itself a trust signal; a dead repo behind a confident
   claim destroys it. (See RECONCILIATION.md §C/§F.)
3. **Reframe "pre-1.0" as RIGOR, not incompleteness.** The reason things aren't 1.0 is the
   standard is high: normative specs, conformance suites, "no output better than wrong
   output", 100%-coverage bars, reproducible benchmarks. Show the discipline → "pre-1.0"
   reads as *serious and careful*, not *unfinished*. Honesty + rigor = the brand.
4. **Show momentum, not just inventory.** Recent releases, changelogs, roadmaps, "what
   shipped this quarter." A lab visibly in motion reads as alive and reliable, not stalled.
5. **Anchor the firm in 15 years of DELIVERY, not just the lab.** The client services
   business has shipped real enterprise work since 2010 (ISO 9001/27001). The Labs is the
   R&D engine on top of a proven delivery company — so "can they ship?" is already answered
   by the company's history; the labs are upside, not the whole bet.
6. **Case studies = the ultimate antidote.** Real client outcomes (the pending input from
   Aamir) prove shipping better than any repo. Highest-priority content.
7. **⭐ The "dogfood-first" maturation model — reframes the whole concern.** (Aamir,
   confirmed 2026-06: *"we use our own products internally first, then release to the
   market."*) Many ManiarTech projects are "internal / not yet public" **not because
   they're unfinished or abandoned, but because the company deliberately runs them on its
   OWN real projects until they're proven, THEN open-sources / goes to market.** This is a
   coherent, repeated operating model — and it flips Risk 1 on its head:
   - "Internal" = **battle-tested on our own work**, not vaporware. (Booster already runs
     across MULTIPLE internal projects incl. Tallery Gallery; MDKit, gowork, gocurl, AddressQL
     all follow the same internal→public path; Tallery & Processious dogfood the tooling.)
   - It's a **quality signal, not a delay**: "we trust it with our own products before we
     ask you to" — earned credibility, the opposite of fake-it.
   - It makes the honest status labels (Internal / Research / Shipping soon) read as
     **stages of a disciplined pipeline**, not excuses.
   → SITE USE: state the model explicitly (an "How we build → dogfood, then release" line on
   About / Labs). It's TRUE, it's differentiated, and it's the single cleanest answer to
   "why is so much pre-1.0/internal?" Pair with the rigor story (Tallery's process,
   signals' honest benchmarks). NB: still never imply an internal tool is publicly
   available — the model is the point, not a publish claim.

> Net: don't pretend everything is 1.0. Show a proven delivery company running a rigorous
> research lab — published work as proof, pipeline as upside, rigor as the reason for the
> pace.

## RISK 2 — "It nearly all traces to one person (Aamir)."
**The silent fear:** *"Is this a company, or one remarkable individual? What happens if
he's busy / unavailable / hit by a bus? Can it scale beyond him?"* This is the #1 unspoken
question of any enterprise buyer or senior hire. Aamir's depth is the superpower AND the risk.

### How the site answers it (antidotes)
1. **Make the founder a deliberate ASSET, not a hidden liability.** A real, substantive
   founder presence (engineer-founder, since 2010, author of the standards/Indigo). For the
   right buyer, a deeply technical, accountable founder *increases* trust. Don't hide him —
   frame him as the proof of engineering depth. (Faceless = more anxiety, not less.)
2. **But show it's a TEAM and a SYSTEM, not a solo act.** Surface the people and the
   process: team page (even if small — quality over headcount), ISO-certified **processes**
   (delivery is "predictable by process, not heroics"), contributors, careers ("we're
   growing"). The honest framing: *small, senior team led by a founder-engineer* — a
   boutique lab, not a one-man show.
3. **Process as the bus-factor answer.** ISO 9001 (quality) + 27001 (security) are exactly
   the "it doesn't depend on one person's memory" proof. Lean on documented process,
   normative specs, conformance tests, "100% coverage" — these show the *work* is captured
   in systems, transferable, not locked in one head.
   - **★ ELEVATE THIS (Aamir, 2026-06): the RARITY is the point.** A boutique team carrying
     BOTH ISO 9001 + 27001 is genuinely rare — these are an ongoing *burden* (documented
     processes, annual surveillance audits, 3-yr recertification, real cost) that most sub-5
     companies never attempt or can't sustain. So it's the **single cleanest proof for BOTH
     trust fears at once**: reliability (quality by process, not heroics) AND bus-factor (work
     in audited systems, not one head). Turns "small" into a flex: **boutique by size,
     enterprise by discipline.** Feature it as a top trust element on Home + About, not a
     footer badge.
   - **Framing (Governing Rule #1):** lean on the CONTRAST, NOT a headcount. *"Most companies
     our size don't carry ISO 9001 and ISO 27001. We do — because process is how we make
     delivery predictable, not heroic."* Never print "< 5 people" (triggers the too-small fear).
   - ⚠️ **HARD-CLAIM CHECK (must verify before featuring):** (a) both certs **currently valid**
     (surveillance up to date, not lapsed); (b) **version** — notes say 27001:**2013**, but the
     world transitioned to **27001:2022** (deadline late 2025) → must read **2022** if certified;
     printing :2013 in 2026 is outdated + catchable. Confirm certifying body + cert numbers
     (naming them = trust via specificity). [RECONCILIATION + pending input.]
4. **"Growing" = momentum, not smallness.** Careers, active hiring, open contribution —
   frame the size as a trajectory. A small team shipping this much reads as *force*.
5. **Don't over-claim headcount.** Never imply a big faceless enterprise — one inflated
   number discredits everything (see north-star: trust via specificity). Own the boutique
   positioning: *deep, senior, founder-led, scaling deliberately.*

> Net: turn the founder into a credibility anchor, then prove there's a team, a process, and
> a system around him so the depth doesn't read as a single point of failure.

## Guiding line: "Show it till they see it" — NOT "fake it till you make it"
The fabrication kind of "fake it till you make it" is BANNED for this brand — it's uniquely
lethal here: (1) we fight an offshore TRUST GAP → held to a higher evidentiary bar, buyers
WILL probe, one exposed lie confirms the prejudice and poisons every claim; (2) our entire
MOAT is honesty (honest-engineering ethos) → faking destroys the one durable differentiator;
(3) ISO/rigor positioning can't coexist with a faked portfolio. AND we don't need it: the
problem is UNDER-selling real substance, not lacking substance. "Fake it" is for the
empty-substance founder; ManiarTech already made it and just isn't showing it.
**LEGITIMATE (do):** bold confident framing of the TRUTH ("larger than life without lying"),
design that punches above headcount, honest "shipping soon" labels, anonymized-but-real case
studies, owning the boutique-lab positioning, projecting where we're going.
**FABRICATION (never):** fake clients/case-studies/testimonials, invented metrics, bought
stars/reviews, "production-ready" when it's beta, pretending to be a big-team enterprise.
Every copy/design decision passes this line: bold framing of real substance = yes;
manufacturing substance that isn't there = no.

## Aspiration vs track record — "Labs prove the ceiling, case studies prove delivery"
Tension (Aamir): he wants to win AMBITIOUS/frontier projects, but his case studies are of
more modest engagements — a gap between envisioned work and track record. RESOLUTION (not
fabrication): two different proofs for two different buyer beliefs —
- **Case studies → "they DELIVER"** (reliability, real outcomes, they ship).
- **Labs/standards/Indigo + Products → "they can handle work THIS HARD"** (capability ceiling).
A normal agency's credible ceiling = its biggest case study. ManiarTech's ceiling = its
HARDEST research project → it can honestly aim far above its case-study scope. The Labs let
us **prove capability WITHOUT needing a matching client engagement** — the key gift.
Honest line: "we CAN build X" (provable via Labs/products/skills) = fair; "we HAVE built X for
[marquee client]" = only if true. Offering a service you can genuinely deliver ≠ faking.
SITE IMPLICATIONS: for ambitious buyers, lead with capability proof (Labs); case studies
back reliability. Frame case studies for TRANSFERABLE DEPTH (the "hard part" / judgment), not
literal scope. Use Processious (enterprise BPA/ERP) as capability proof too. Set
services/positioning at the level the Labs+team can actually deliver (genuinely high). RISK:
avoid dissonance — don't inflate case studies to match a frontier-lab positioning; instead let
the Labs carry "we operate at the frontier" and case studies carry "and we deliver reliably."
