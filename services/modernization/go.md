---
heading: "Moving to Go - when the workload actually benefits"
---

A specific part of a system buckling under load - high concurrency, throughput, or compute cost - is often what starts a modernization. Go is a strong answer for those: concurrency, performance-critical paths, lower compute cost, and single-binary deployment. So when the workload genuinely benefits, we migrate to Go.

When it does not, we say so and modernize on the stack that fits. We build in Python too, and we have modernized a system of our own onto newer Python precisely because that was the right call for it. We are not selling you a language - we are solving your problem, and the destination follows the workload, not a house preference.
