# UpSport — an in-browser video-review studio

**Status: Delivered (2019)**

> A coach's video-review studio that, in 2019, ran where it arguably shouldn't have been able to —
> entirely inside the browser: record commentary and **draw live on the running video**, captured in
> real time. Built for **Jeff Hines** — a founder who'd already worked with us — for his next venture.

## The situation

This wasn't a cold call. **Jeff Hines** had spent years watching us engineer his previous company's
platform, **Touchpoint Dashboard** — and when he started his next venture, **UpSport**, he came back
to ManiarTech. A founder choosing the same engineering team a second time is about the clearest vote of
confidence we get.

His idea was a hard one. Coaches needed a way to review player video and actually *coach* through it —
not just watch footage, but mark it up, talk over it, and hand a player something they could learn
from. He wanted it to live online, in the browser, so a coach could open a player's match video
anywhere and go straight to work. The question was whether the browser of 2019 could carry it.

## What we built

A small ManiarTech team — two people, with our own architect leading the core and the hardest parts —
built the platform from scratch.

The heart of it was an **in-browser video-review editor**. A coach plays a player's uploaded match
footage and works on it live: **records voice commentary**, **draws directly on the running video** to
point out exactly what to fix, sets markers and comments at the moments that matter, and slows the
video down or speeds it up to study a movement. And the part that made it hard — **all of it, including
the drawing on the moving video, is captured live, in the browser.**

In 2019, that sat at the edge of what browsers could do, so we treated it like a frontier rather than a
feature. We built a **proof-of-concept first**, to prove the browser could actually support live
capture and live annotation *before* committing to the full build. For the live capture we used
**WebRTC**; for drawing on the running video, we used **WebDoodling — our own canvas framework.** We
made sure the technology could go far enough before we promised it would.

## Why it matters

Two things. First, the **returning founder**: Jeff Hines had already seen us engineer one of his
platforms, and he chose us again for the next. A client who comes back is the strongest proof that the
first time went right. Second, **frontier capability**: when a requirement sits at the edge of what's
possible, we de-risk it with a proof-of-concept and build on technology we make ourselves — rather than
promising and hoping. The live draw-on-video studio is a real example of pushing the browser further
than it was expected to go, and proving it first.
