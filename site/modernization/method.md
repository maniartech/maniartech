---
heading: "How we de-risk it - piece by piece, never all at once"
steps:
  - name: "Audit and plan"
    what: "We inventory what you have - dependencies, risks, what the system actually does - and decide with you what is worth migrating and what should stay. Sometimes the honest answer is to leave a part alone."
    gives: "A migration plan you can challenge before anything is touched."
  - name: "One module at a time"
    what: "We migrate a piece at a time, not the whole thing at once, so the risk at any moment stays small and easy to see."
    gives: "Progress you can watch, in increments you can stop."
  - name: "Old and new, side by side"
    what: "The existing system keeps serving while the modern one comes up behind it, gated by feature flags so traffic can move across gradually rather than in one leap."
    gives: "Uptime through the whole migration, not just after it."
  - name: "Every step reversible"
    what: "If a step misbehaves we roll it back. There is no single point of no return and no go-live weekend where everything rides on one cutover."
    gives: "A way back at each step, so no move is a gamble."
  - name: "Verified as we go"
    what: "Each migrated piece ships with its own tests and checks, so modernized also means proven, not something you find out about later in production."
    gives: "Confidence that what moved still does what it did."
---

The fear is not the code. It is the all-at-once rewrite that runs late, runs over, and breaks the business the day it goes live. Our method is the opposite of that gamble. This is a real strangler-fig migration discipline - the modern system grows inside the old one and takes over the load gradually, until the old one can be retired. It is the same method we run on our own systems, in the open.
