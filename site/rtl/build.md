## What we built

We rebuilt the system on WPF (.NET). The real work was not porting it - it was streamlining it.

- **600+ forms and reports became one.** We replaced the entire form sprawl with a single, workflow-driven screen and a single report. The screen is organised around the sample's journey: open it and staff see, at a glance, exactly where every sample stands - created, in lab, analysis complete, checked, signed once, signed twice, report dispatched, invoice authorized, invoice dispatched.
- **Role-based access.** Who can check, who can sign, who can authorise an invoice - access is governed by role, so the right people do the right steps.
- **Less chasing.** Built-in notifications and requisitions removed the manual follow-up the old system forced on people.
- **Integrated accounts.** We brought the accounts and invoicing system into the same application, so the lab and its billing ran as one.
- **An XPS reporting engine.** Built on the document technology already inside .NET, it produced high-quality analytical reports, label printing, and more.
- **A dark UI - in 2011.** We built it dark a decade before that became common, so analysts could work long hours with less eye strain.

Later, the lab came back to extend the system to the web. We built an ASP.NET portal that let the lab's own clients download their analytical reports directly from RTL's website - turning report delivery into self-service, in a second phase of the engagement.

In total we were engaged for around two years, and the result was one application running the lab end to end: sample login, analysis, multi-step review and signing, dispatch, invoicing, and reporting.
