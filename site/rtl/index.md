> A prominent Mumbai analytical lab had outgrown its own record system. We rebuilt it into a single, workflow-driven application that — by the lab's account — is still running today.

## The situation

Reliable Analytical Laboratories is one of Mumbai's prominent analytical testing labs. It runs thousands of tests, grouped in series, each with many parameters to check against requirements, through a genuinely complex pipeline: a sample is received, logged, accepted by the lab, analysed, reviewed, signed off more than once, then dispatched and invoiced.

The lab's own team had built a record-tracking system in-house, in Microsoft Access. Over years of growth it had become substantial — more than 600 forms, about as many reports, and a sprawl of data tables, wrapped around the lab's real workflows. It captured a great deal and reflected a deep understanding of the work. But it had outgrown the tool it was built in: it was slow, and hard to learn and to train new staff on.

This wasn't a case of replacing a failed vendor system. It was taking a deeply understood internal system — one the lab's own people knew end to end — and making it fast, learnable, and built to last. We worked hand in hand with the team that knew every workflow in it.

## What we built

We rebuilt the system on WPF (.NET). The real work wasn't porting it — it was streamlining it.

- **600+ forms and reports became one.** We replaced the entire form sprawl with a single, workflow-driven screen and a single report. The screen is organised around the sample's journey: open it and staff see, at a glance, exactly where every sample stands — created, in lab, analysis complete, checked, signed, report dispatched, invoiced.
- **Role-based access.** Who can check, who can sign, who can authorise an invoice — access is governed by role, so the right people do the right steps.
- **Integrated accounts.** We brought the accounts and invoicing system into the same application, so the lab and its billing ran as one.
- **An XPS reporting engine.** Built on the .NET document technology, it produced high-quality reports, label printing, and more.
- **A dark UI — in 2011.** We built it dark a decade before it became common, so analysts could work long hours with less eye strain.

Later, the lab came back to extend the system to the web. We built a .NET web portal that let the lab's own clients download their analytical reports directly from the lab's website — turning report delivery into self-service.

## Where it stands

By the lab's account, the application is still in production today — around fifteen years on — with some of their own later modifications. We understand it continues to run the lab's day-to-day work. This is secondhand; we are no longer in direct contact with the lab, so we state it as the lab reports it rather than as a firm claim.

## Why it matters

The best test of how software is built is how long it keeps working. Two lines tell the story: 600 forms became one, and — by the lab's account — it's still running around fifteen years later. That's a small team shipping something solid that lasts, long after most software of its era would have been replaced.
