## 4. Voice & tone
Calm, senior, precise, honest. We sound like a principal engineer who respects the reader's time —
not a marketing department. No hype, no unbacked superlatives, no jargon-as-drama.

**Understated maker-tone (applies especially to Foundry — products, standards, languages, tools).**
Building our own technology is a *matter-of-fact part of life at ManiarTech* — patient craft, not a
trophy. Present it plainly and let the work speak. AVOID: "Yes — we build X!", "look how great we
are", "most companies use X, we author it" superiority framing, triumphant openers. PREFER: plain
statements of fact ("We've built two languages."), quiet confidence, "we build things like these when
the work calls for them." Proof (benchmarks, live URLs, repos) is welcome — that's evidence, not
boasting — as long as it's calibrated and sourced.

**Presentation Doctrine (ordering):** lead with the buyer's outcome and safety; put the depth /
"we make technology" story LAST, as the closer — never the front door (except on depth-first
audiences like Labs/Standards, where depth may lead).

## 5. Positioning (DECIDED)
- **Depth / quality / world-class engineering.** A research-driven engineering lab that authors
  standards and a language, and builds with senior engineers.
- **No price claim as a brand promise** — never "cheap" OR "premium-priced." Price is handled
  per-engagement (estimator/proposals). Efficiency is a benefit clients discover, not a billboard.
- **Honesty about the team:** present the senior-only STANDARD + the "building the best team"
  AMBITION. Do NOT claim a large world-class team already exists.
- Primary CTA everywhere: **"Estimate your project →"**. Secondary: "Talk to a human."

## 6. Governing Rule #1 — honesty (absolute)
Every claim TRUE + SOURCED + CALIBRATED. No invented numbers, logos, names, or dates. Use honest
status labels (Published · Beta · In production · In development · Early-stage · Research · Internal).
Keep links "dark" where the work isn't public — never a dead "coming soon" link.
